# 1. Executive Summary

This report documents Phase 1 of a course project for CSE 347/652 | DES 306/525 Usable Security and Privacy (IIIT-Delhi, Monsoon 2026). The project addresses authentication and authorization at the campus entry gate. Today, entry is controlled by a physical college ID card that a security guard inspects by eye. The card proves possession, not identity: a lost, stolen, borrowed, forged, expired, or revoked card is visually identical to a valid one, and a guard working a busy gate has seconds to decide.

Phase 1 follows the course structure: **user studies → requirements → design → workflows → prototype**. We designed a three-method primary research plan (a student questionnaire, semi-structured interviews with security staff, and a non-interfering contextual inquiry at the gate), completed a literature and existing-system review, built a threat model with twelve threat actors and seventeen threats, compared QR, RFID and NFC against sixteen criteria, wrote a formal requirements catalogue (18 functional, 20 security, 12 privacy, 14 usability, 12 non-functional requirements), designed the credential lifecycle, security and system architecture, data-flow and privacy data-flow diagrams, and produced a twenty-screen lo-fi prototype with an initial heuristic evaluation and a full traceability matrix.

**Status of primary data.** The primary research instruments, consent statement, data-collection template, and analysis framework are complete. Fieldwork results are **not** included in this version. Every place where primary evidence belongs is marked **[DATA REQUIRED]** or with a specific placeholder such as **[INSERT ACTUAL NUMBER]**. No participant counts, percentages, averages, quotes, or observations have been invented. Sections that rest on secondary research or on explicitly labelled design assumptions are marked as such.

**Provisional technology direction.** Based on the threat model and secondary research, the design direction is a *hybrid* credential: a cryptographically authenticated contactless credential (ISO/IEC 14443 NFC-class, either a smart card with challenge-response or a phone-based NFC credential) as the primary path, a short-lived signed QR code generated by a student mobile app as the secondary path, and time-bound signed QR passes for visitors. Static QR codes and low-frequency 125 kHz RFID were rejected as a sole primary mechanism because they are trivially copied. This direction is provisional until primary data on device ownership, acceptance, and gate conditions is collected.

**Security depth.** The design treats authentication ("who is this credential bound to?") and authorization ("is this principal allowed through this gate now?") as separate decisions with separate failure messages, applies fail-secure behaviour for network and database outages (a bounded, signed offline revocation list rather than an open gate), enforces least privilege and role-based visibility (the guard sees a photo, first name, role, and a decision, never a phone number or address), minimises stored personal data, and makes every access and administrative decision auditable in an append-only log. No custom cryptography is proposed; Phase 2 will use established primitives and libraries only.

# 2. Problem Statement

Access to the IIIT-Delhi campus is controlled at the gate by a security guard who visually checks a physical college identity card. The card carries a printed photo, name, roll number, programme, and validity date. In practice the guard decides in a few seconds, often while several people wait, whether the card looks genuine and whether the photo resembles the holder.

This process authenticates the *card*, not the *person*, and it conflates two different questions:

| Question | Meaning | How the current system answers it |
|---|---|---|
| **Authentication** — "Who are you?" | Is this credential genuine and bound to this individual? | Guard compares printed photo to face; judges whether the card looks real |
| **Authorization** — "Are you allowed to enter here, now?" | Is this individual currently permitted at this gate? | Guard assumes any current-looking card implies permission |

The failure modes that follow from this design are the problems this project addresses:

- **Lost or stolen cards** remain usable by whoever holds them until the card physically expires, because there is no revocation check at the gate.
- **Borrowed cards**: a student can hand a card to a friend or outsider; photo comparison under time pressure is unreliable.
- **Forged or altered cards**: a printed card with a laminate can be replicated with consumer equipment.
- **Expired cards** are frequently accepted because the date is small and rarely read.
- **Revoked status** (a suspended, withdrawn, or graduated student) is not visible on the card at all.
- **Visitors** are handled by a separate manual register that is slow, error-prone, and stores personal information on paper.
- **Manual verification errors** in both directions: legitimate students refused, unauthorised people admitted.
- **Queues and delay** at peak times (morning classes, hostel curfew), which increases pressure to wave people through.
- **No reliable audit trail**: there is no record of who entered when, which hampers incident response.
- **Privacy**: the card exposes roll number, programme and sometimes more to anyone who sees it, and visitor registers expose phone numbers and addresses to anyone standing near the desk.
- **No defined behaviour for failures**: if lighting is poor, the guard is new, or the gate is short-staffed, there is no fallback procedure other than personal judgement.

The problem, stated precisely: **the campus lacks a mechanism that (a) authenticates the individual rather than the artefact, (b) checks current authorization at the point of entry, (c) does so in a few seconds under queue pressure, (d) fails securely when infrastructure fails, and (e) exposes only the minimum personal information needed to make the decision.**

# 3. Motivation

**Security motivation.** Physical access control is the outer perimeter of every other security control on campus: laboratories, hostels, data centres, examination halls. A perimeter that accepts any plausible-looking card provides little assurance. Published research on deployed access credentials shows that "looks secure" and "is secure" diverge sharply: MIFARE Classic cards used in buildings and transit were shown to be cloneable in 2008 [4, 5], and hotel lock systems covering millions of doors were shown in 2018 and 2024 to be openable from a single discarded or read keycard [8, 9]. A campus system designed without a threat model will repeat these mistakes.

**Usability motivation.** The usable-security literature consistently finds that security mechanisms that add friction are circumvented by the very people they protect, and that the circumvention is rational, not careless [10, 11]. A gate that takes 15 seconds per person at 8:55 a.m. will be bypassed by the guard. Any design must therefore be as fast as, or faster than, the current glance-at-a-card.

**Privacy motivation.** An electronic gate necessarily creates a record of movement. Without deliberate data minimisation and retention limits, this becomes a surveillance dataset of every student's daily pattern. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 [22] establishes purpose limitation and data minimisation as legal obligations for data fiduciaries, and the campus would be one.

**Course motivation.** The project is a vehicle for demonstrating that security, privacy and usability can be designed in from the start rather than bolted on. The Phase 1 deliverables (user studies, requirements, prototype) exist precisely to show the evidence chain from user need to design decision before any code is written.

# 4. Project Objectives

**Primary objective.** Design a campus entry authentication system that provides strong authentication and authorization while protecting user privacy, resisting common attacks, handling failures securely, and remaining fast and easy to use for students and security staff.

| O8 | Demonstrate traceability from user finding to prototype screen to future test | Section 37, Appendix F |

**Qualities the design must demonstrate**: strong security, usability, privacy, functional correctness, reliability, accessibility, and human-centred design, with the greatest depth in security.

# 5. Scope

**In scope for Phase 1**

- Entry authentication and authorization at campus perimeter gates (pedestrian and, conceptually, vehicle gates).
- Three principal roles: students, security guards (gate operators), and administrators (security office / ID cell).
- Visitor entry with pre-approval and time-bound credentials.
- Credential lifecycle from issuance to deletion.
- Failure handling: lost, forgotten, expired, revoked, forged and borrowed credentials; reader, network, database and power failure; emergency entry; suspicious repeated attempts.
- Threat model, requirements, architecture, workflows, lo-fi prototype, heuristic evaluation.

**Explicitly out of scope for Phase 1**

- Working software. Phase 1 delivers design artefacts only.
- Building-level or room-level access (labs, hostels), though the architecture should not preclude it.
- Payment, library, mess, or attendance functions of the ID card.
- Biometric enrolment or biometric matching by the system. Photo comparison remains a human task; we discuss biometrics only as a Phase 2 option that would need separate ethical review.
- Procurement decisions or vendor selection.
- Faculty and staff as a separately studied population (they are treated as a role variant of "authorised member" in the requirements, but were not a required study population).

**Assumptions (labelled)**

- **A1**: Gates are staffed during operating hours; the system assists guards rather than replacing them.
- **A2**: Mains power and campus network are normally available at gates but cannot be assumed to be always available.
- **A3**: Most, but not all, students carry a smartphone. The proportion is **[DATA REQUIRED]** from the questionnaire; the design must not exclude students without one.
- **A4**: The institution already maintains an authoritative student roster (admissions/registrar) that can act as the identity source.

# 7. Current System

**Description (based on team observation of the publicly visible process and general knowledge of the campus; specifics to be confirmed by the contextual inquiry — [DATA REQUIRED]).**

- **Credential**: a printed, laminated photo ID card issued at admission. It displays photo, name, roll number, programme, and a validity period. Some cards include a barcode or magnetic stripe used for library or other services; whether the gate uses any machine-readable feature is **[DATA REQUIRED]**.
- **Verification**: a guard at the pedestrian gate visually inspects the card. At vehicle gates a sticker or the same card is shown through the window.
- **Decision**: the guard admits, questions, or refuses the person. There is no electronic record of the decision.
- **Exceptions**: a student without a card may be asked for another form of ID, asked to name a hostel or department, or refused. The actual procedure is **[DATA REQUIRED]**.
- **Visitors**: sign a paper register (name, phone number, person visited, purpose, time in/out) and may deposit a government ID or receive a visitor badge. Details **[DATA REQUIRED]**.
- **Revocation**: when a card is reported lost, a replacement is issued; there is no mechanism to prevent the lost card being used at the gate, since the gate performs no lookup.
- **Logging**: none for students; paper register for visitors.

**Properties of the current system against the project's qualities**

| Quality | Current system |
|---|---|
| Authentication strength | Low: possession of a plausible-looking card; photo match under time pressure |
| Authorization check | None distinct from authentication; any current card implies permission |
| Revocation | Not enforced at gate |
| Expiry | Printed date; rarely read |
| Audit | None for students; paper for visitors |
| Speed | Fast when uncontested (a glance); slow when guard questions the holder. Actual timings **[DATA REQUIRED]** |
| Privacy | Card exposes roll number and programme to bystanders; visitor register exposes phone numbers to anyone near the desk |
| Failure handling | Undefined; depends on individual guard |
| Accessibility | Card is device-independent, which is a strength; but a student with a motor impairment may find retrieving a card from a bag difficult, and there is no non-visual feedback |

# 8. Current Workflow

The current-state workflow, as the team understands it before fieldwork. Steps marked ⚠ are where the contextual inquiry (Appendix D) must confirm or correct the description.

```
Student approaches gate
        |
        v
Retrieves ID card from bag / pocket / lanyard          ⚠ time taken varies
        |
        v
Shows card to guard (may be waved at a distance)       ⚠ distance & attention
        |
        v
Guard glances at photo, card colour/design             ⚠ what does the guard actually look at?
        |
        +-- Looks fine ------------> Nod / gesture --> Student enters (no record)
        |
        +-- Uncertain -------------> Guard asks student to stop
        |                                  |
        |                                  +-- Asks question (hostel? dept?)  ⚠ what questions?
        |                                  +-- Compares photo more closely
        |                                  +-- Calls supervisor               ⚠ how often?
        |                                          |
        |                                          +-- Admit
        |                                          +-- Refuse
        |
        +-- No card ---------------> Student explains
                                           |
                                           +-- Guard recognises student -> admit  ⚠ informal trust
                                           +-- Alternative ID / call to dept      ⚠
                                           +-- Refuse / wait for friend with card
```

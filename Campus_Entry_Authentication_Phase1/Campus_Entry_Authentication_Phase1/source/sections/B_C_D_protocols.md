# Appendix B. Security Guard Interview Protocol

**Format:** Semi-structured, 20–30 minutes, one interviewer and one note-taker. Conducted off-shift or during a quiet period, with permission from the security supervisor. Language: Hindi or English as the participant prefers; the protocol should be translated verbally where needed and the translation noted.

## B.1 Introduction (read aloud)

> Namaste / Hello. Thank you for speaking with us. We are students in a course on usable security at IIIT-Delhi. We are studying how campus entry works so that any future system is designed around the people who actually operate it. We want to learn from your experience; there are no right or wrong answers, and we are not evaluating you or your work. This will take about 20–30 minutes.

## B.2 Consent statement (read aloud; see also Appendix C)

> Your participation is voluntary. You can skip any question or stop at any time, and this will have no effect on your job. We will not record your name, employee ID, or which gate you work at in our report. We will take written notes. We will only record audio if you say yes, and we will delete the audio after we write it up. What you tell us will be combined with what other guards tell us and reported as a group, so no one can be identified. Do you agree to take part? [Record: Yes / No] May we record audio? [Yes / No]

## B.3 Core questions

| # | Question | Topic | Follow-up probes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Can you walk me through what you do when a student comes to the gate, from the moment you see them to when they're through? | Current workflow | What do you look at first? How long does it usually take? Does it differ at the vehicle gate? |
| Q2 | How do you decide the ID is genuine? | Verification | What features do you look for? Have you seen cards that looked wrong? What did you do? |
| Q3 | How often do you recognise students by face rather than checking the card? | Verification / workaround | Is that more common at certain times? Do you feel that is reliable? |
| Q4 | Do you check the validity or expiry date on the card? | Expiry | How easy is it to read? What happens if it's expired? |
| Q5 | What do you do if the photo doesn't seem to match the person, or the card looks suspicious? | Suspicious / fake IDs | Who do you call? How long does it take? How often does this happen? |
| Q6 | What happens when a student arrives without an ID card? | Forgotten ID | Is there a set procedure? Does it vary by guard or shift? What do students usually say? |
| Q7 | If a student has lost their card, how would you know if someone else is using it? | Lost / stolen | Is there any list or notice you receive? |
| Q8 | Tell me about the busiest times. What changes in how you check? | Busy periods / delays | How long do queues get? Do you ever have to speed up checks? What would help most at those times? |
| Q9 | How do you handle visitors? | Visitors | What information do you write down? Who checks it? What happens with the register afterwards? What is hardest about visitors? |
| Q10 | Have you used any electronic device or system at a gate here or elsewhere, e.g., a card reader, scanner, or tablet? What was your experience? | Device / network | What went wrong? What happened when the device or network failed? Was there a fallback? |
| Q11 | If a screen showed you information about the person at the gate, what would you need to see to make a confident decision? | Information needed | Photo? Name? Roll number? Department? Hostel? Anything else? |
| Q12 | Is there information about students that you think should *not* be shown to you, or that you'd be uncomfortable being responsible for? | Privacy | Phone numbers? Addresses? Why? |
| Q13 | What training did you receive for gate duty, and how long did it take to feel confident? | Training | What would a new guard find hardest? |
| Q14 | If you could change one thing about how entry works, what would it be? | Desired features | Why? What would you be worried about with an electronic system? |

# Appendix D. Contextual Inquiry (Observation) Protocol

**Purpose.** Establish ground truth for how entry actually happens: step count, timing, queue behaviour, guard actions, exceptions, and environmental constraints. Interviews tell us what people believe they do; observation shows what happens.

**Type.** Non-participant, non-interfering observation in a public area. Observers do not speak to people in the queue, do not handle cards, do not photograph or film, and do not ask guards questions during the session (questions go to the interview).

## D.1 Pre-conditions

- Written or e-mail permission from the security supervisor; inform the guards on shift before starting.
- Observers wear visible college IDs and stand where the supervisor agrees, out of the flow.
- Stop immediately if asked by security staff or if an incident occurs.

## D.2 Sessions

| Session | Target window | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Morning peak (e.g., 08:30–09:15) | Maximum load; queue formation; speed pressure |
| S2 | Off-peak (e.g., 14:30–15:15) | Baseline behaviour without pressure |
| S3 | Evening / hostel return (e.g., 21:30–22:15) | Low light; different guard shift; visitor sign-out |
| S4 (optional) | Second gate, any window | Compare gates |

Two observers per session: **Observer A** times individuals; **Observer B** writes field notes and counts queue length every 2 minutes.

## D.3 Timing method

- Use a stopwatch app with lap function.
- **T0**: person reaches the guard's position (front of queue). **T1**: person passes the gate line or is turned away. Record T1–T0 in seconds.
- Classify each timed person as: **U** (uncontested: card shown or not, no stop), **Q** (questioned: guard stops and speaks), **N** (no card visible), **V** (visitor), **R** (refused).
- Time at least 30 consecutive people per session, or the whole session if fewer.
- Record queue length (number waiting behind the person at the guard) at 2-minute intervals.

## D.4 Observation checklist (Observer B)

**Environment**
- [ ] Lighting (adequate / dim / glare)
- [ ] Weather / shelter for guard and queue
- [ ] Noise level
- [ ] Position of guard relative to flow; is there a desk, booth, barrier, turnstile?
- [ ] Power outlet / network equipment visible?
- [ ] Space available for a reader or display; would a queue block it?

**Process**
- [ ] How does the guard signal "go ahead"? (nod, gesture, verbal, nothing)
- [ ] Where is the card when shown? (in hand, on lanyard, waved from distance, not shown)
- [ ] Does the guard ever look at the expiry date? Turn the card over?
- [ ] Does the guard compare photo to face? For how long?
- [ ] Does behaviour change when the queue grows? At what length?
- [ ] Are groups checked individually or as a group?
- [ ] Vehicle gate: what is checked and from what distance?

**Exceptions** (record each occurrence with time, type, duration, outcome)
- [ ] No card
- [ ] Guard questions holder
- [ ] Guard calls someone
- [ ] Refusal
- [ ] Visitor registration: steps, what is written, who writes, time taken, where the register sits and who can read it
- [ ] Any apparent workaround (e.g., friend passing card back, guard waving group)

**Guard workload**
- [ ] Concurrent duties (phone, vehicle barrier, register, radio)
- [ ] Number of guards present
- [ ] Interruptions

## D.5 What to record

- Counts, durations, queue lengths, classification codes (U/Q/N/V/R).
- Environmental conditions and physical constraints.
- Descriptions of *process* and *behaviour* in general terms ("guard asked holder a question", not what was asked if it contained personal detail).
- Time-stamped exceptions and their outcomes.

## D.6 What NOT to record

- Names, roll numbers, faces, photographs, video, audio.
- Card contents, vehicle registration numbers, visitor register contents.
- Identifying details of individual guards (name, badge, appearance).
- Conversations between guard and student beyond their general nature.
- Anything a guard or student asks you not to record.

## D.7 Ethical considerations

- The gate is a public space and the observed behaviour is public; however, individuals have not consented, so the protocol collects only non-identifying aggregate data.
- Guards are informed in advance and may decline; observation of a guard who declines does not proceed.
- Observers must not create the impression of auditing or reporting on individual guards. The consent statement (Appendix C) is available to any guard who asks.
- If observers witness a security incident, they do not intervene and do not record identifying details; they note only "incident occurred, observation paused".
